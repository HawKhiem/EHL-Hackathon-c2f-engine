Problem02

What is hard about pricing a claim from both sides

As the handyman (issuer)

  • You never see t. Charge a cent over it and the charge flips from "paid by all 16" to "paid by the lenient few".
  • The fair value depends on a policy (sub-limits, declared values, exclusions), a market rate, and an invoiced quantity. Three sources of error that compound.
  • One or two items per round are worth more than all the others together. Being 10 % timid on a whale costs more than being wrong on ten call-out fees.

As the insurer (reviewer)

  • Refusing a fair charge costs 1.5x; accepting a fraudulent one costs the full amount. The mistakes are asymmetric and you cannot see which one you are making.
  • Coverage is binary and policy-specific (t = 0 when not covered): the same line item is payable in one case and excluded in the next.
  • 16 opponents bill you every item. Their charges are a signal about t that you only get to read after the round.

And for both: 60 seconds, from key release to board close

Decrypt, read a policy of up to 65k characters and an invoice of up to 39 lines, decide a and b for every line, submit. A model call that takes 33 s against that clock is a missed round. A missed round refuses every fair charge at 1.5x while charging nothing: the single most expensive outcome in the game.

Problem, the payoff03

The payoff, made explicit: everything hinges on where a sits relative to t

Issuer: the payoff is a step

R(a) = a · P(t ≥ a) + 0.5·t · P(t < a)

Measured on the field: revenue per opponent above t is flat at 0.48 to 0.53 t for every overcharge ratio from 1.0 to 5. Crossing t forfeits half at once, and nothing more is gained beyond it.

Reviewer: accept iff P(fair) > ⅔

accept: cost a  ·  refuse: cost 1.5a·P(fair)
⇒ b = Q(⅓) of an honest belief

With coverage inside it: b = sup{ a : P(cov)·S(a) ≥ ⅔ }. Everything hinges on the belief being honest, not on the quantile being tuned.

17 teams, every pair matched both ways, 60 s from key release to board close, ~100 rounds over two days.

Approach, day 104

The strategy we went in with

Let the model read, let the math decide

  • One LLM call reads policy, description and invoice verbatim and returns, per line: covered?, related?, t_low / t_mid / t_high.
  • A lognormal belief around t_mid. The charge maximises mean minus 0.55 sd and never exceeds the median; the limit is the 1/3 quantile.
  • A fast safety pass submits first and the full pass overwrites it. Later: long invoices are split into parallel chunks so one slow call cannot cost the round.

Learn from the leaderboard after every round

  • The public payout feed proves a bracket [t_lo, t_hi) per item: a charge paid by everyone is fair, a charge refused by everyone and paid 0 is fraud.
  • Fit a bias and a sigma between our t_mid and the proven brackets, fit how often reviewers accept over-charges, and re-price every stored round to test a change in seconds.
  • Decide changes on money, expected and pessimistic, never on a single metric.

That skeleton survived to the end. Almost every number inside it was replaced.

Approach, what the data said06

Six things the data told us that we did not expect

63rd pct

t is exogenous. It sits at the 63rd percentile of the field's charges (IQR 0.33 to 0.86). There is a real number to estimate. The market is not the answer key.

0.08vs 0.41

Memory beats the model. For recurring line items, past proven brackets predict t with MAD 0.08 to 0.10 in log space; the LLM 0.36 to 0.41 on the same items. Averaging the two is worse than memory alone.

−0.03

The LLM's interval is uninformative. Its stated q10 to q90 has correlation −0.03 with its realised error. σ must come from measured residuals.

0.5 t

Overcharging is flat. The fitted p₀·r−k curve (k<1) claimed revenue rises forever. That was a censoring artifact. Refusals reconstructed, it is a flat plateau.

2items

Whales decide rounds. The best team's entire lead was two items charged at ~2× the field median and still fair (9,585 vs our 4,771; 8,942 vs 5,285).

−116k

Presence beats pricing. Two rounds with no board cost more than all pricing errors of the same 15-round window combined.

Engine v1: the model prices everything

t_low/t_mid/t_high from the prompt; a 3-segment log-log spline corrects size-dependent bias (β below a pivot, above it, and a whale segment); σ by interval-censored MLE; per-category bias buckets with shrinkage.

Engine v2: memory first, model second

Per-line memory prior from every past proven bracket, precision-weighted against the LLM; a 1,400-character prompt with anchors inline and p_covered instead of a boolean; autoplay launches every round 90 s early.

Autoplay: reads the schedule, never re-plays a logged round, never runs two rounds at once. Being present is the whole job.

Solution, the logic08

The belief is the whole decision. a and b are derived, not tuned

ln t | covered ~ N(μ, σ²)
μ = precision-weighted( memory μ_M/σ_M² , LLM ln q50/σ_L² )
a = argmax_a P(cov)·[ a·S(a) + 0.5·E[t ; t<a] ] (capped at Q(0.55))
b = exp( μ + σ·Φ⁻¹(1 − ⅔ / P(cov)) )
whale (q50 ≥ 2k): b ≥ posterior median
  • Why pure EV, not mean − λ·sd: a round is a portfolio of 10 to 20 items × 16 opponents; per-item shading forfeited 21 to 40 % of provably collectable income. Re-priced regret fell 748,539 to 516,256 (−31 %).
  • Why B_QUANTILE = ⅓ is derived: the tuned values (0.33 to 0.40) were patches for a mis-centred belief. Centre μ instead (live_shift, reliability-monitored) and the quantile is the number the math gives.
  • Why memory is price, not coverage: a past refusal predicts the next case's coverage only 40 % of the time. Policies vary per case; market prices do not.

Solution, market memory09

The market remembers what it paid. We made that the prior.

  • Precision-weighted, so memory dominates where it exists (σ 0.15 vs 0.6); the LLM carries novel items and whales.
  • Anchors go inline per item in the prompt, for example "paid ~EUR 58 to 86 per line when covered (n=11)", not one generic history block.
  • Indemnity lines (stolen goods, declared values) take memory only as a floor: another case's watch says nothing about this one.

The v2 prompt, in full spirit

For every line: p_covered (0 to 1, calibrated: p≥0.9: 42 covered, 3 not; p<0.1: 0 covered, 6 not), honest q10 / q50 / q90, a 12-word reason. Policy limits bind. Whales first. 1,400 characters; three parallel samples; first to land is submitted as insurance, the per-item median overwrites it.

Solution, learning loop11

Every bad round produced a measurement, not an opinion

Truth from public payouts

Each round's feed proves brackets: a charge paid by all is fair (t ≥ a); a charge refused by all and paid 0 is fraud (t < a). Matchup cells tighten them. 47 to 96 truth files.

Calibration with a drift alarm

Interval-censored MLE of bias and σ on current-prompt estimates only; a live_shift fitted on live rounds; make learn prints the measured P(t ≥ Q(⅓)) against the ⅔ the rule needs.

Replay, score, decide

Stored estimates are re-priced in seconds; a change ships when it wins on money in a majority of rounds and on the two-sided counterfactual, never on a one-sided metric.

What the loop caught

  • The belief's reliability flipped sign between prompt eras: claimed P(t≥b) 0.60, measured [0.67, 0.76] on the old prompt and [0.43, 0.43] live on the new one. Seven knobs were patching the same residual on different windows.
  • Widening σ to the measured residual (≈1.15) loses money (−80k leave-one-out): the decision-relevant σ is not the residual σ, because the payoff is asymmetric and bounded.
  • The "accept everything" idea: a team with a 93 % accept rate broke even and paid 25k in fraud in one round. The winning teams accept the right charges.
Post-mortem12

Where the money actually went (rounds 30 to 44: −165k)

The one that taught us the most: round 54, −48.6k

Game 7's air-con unit was refused as not covered (t < 80.83). v2's memory read that ceiling as a price band of EUR 47 to 77; the prompt made memory binding; the model obeyed: q50 = 62 on a EUR 2,000 unit, copied onto its sibling. b = 54 then refused every fair charge on the two biggest items.

Fix (v4): memory carries price from paid observations only; refusals never lower coverage; anchors advisory and quantity-aware. Same estimates, math only: +17 %; fresh replay turned −48.6k into −10.7k on that round.

Verdict13

Why we succeeded, and why we did not

Succeeded

  • The measured-payoff maths paid immediately and measurably: step objective, derived b, coverage inside the rule: 31 % less regret re-priced, +49k / +18k on the counterfactual rounds.
  • Market memory was the single best idea: 4× the model's precision on recurring lines, three firsts in v2's first six rounds, at a fifth of the latency.
  • The learning loop was real (truth, calibration, reliability alarm, replay), so fixes were validated before they went live, and mistakes produced numbers.

Did not

  • Operations cost more than modelling: ~120k to missed rounds and toolchain breakage; autoplay came late.
  • The LLM's level error on first-sight items (+0.36, MAD 0.8 live) is the residual we never fixed. No constant can fix it, only more memory coverage or a prior for novel items.
  • We tuned on windows that moved under us (replay vs live, old vs new prompt) several times before building the live-only monitor.
  • Several engines in one working tree across machines: artefact collisions on every pull, and two machines once played the same round.
